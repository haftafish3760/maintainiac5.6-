Batch 2026-06-27 maintenance UI/UX requirements:

- Work on maintenance UI/UX first. Do not wire Firebase, backend persistence,
  notification permissions, or expense behavior during this pass.
- Icons are last. Do not spend this pass replacing maintenance artwork.
- The first-time maintenance flow starts with the tracked-item selection screen.
  After the user taps Continue, the app must land at the top of the maintenance
  home screen.
- The maintenance app must look like the rest of Maintainiac, not like a
  cartoon. Avoid bright oversized borders, heavy outlines, and loud container
  styling except where color is functional.
- Use the app's existing dark industrial surfaces, thin structural borders,
  compact labels, filled readable controls, and consistent spacing.
- Layout must be responsive. Put related fields side by side whenever feasible
  on normal phone widths and larger screens. Stack them only when the device is
  too narrow, such as an iPhone SE class layout.
- The active vehicle row/header is the vehicle selector. Do not make the
  maintenance vehicle overview act as the vehicle chooser.
- Maintenance item setup/detail screens must show the active vehicle header so
  the user knows which vehicle's mileage and maintenance record they are editing.
- Vehicle overview on the maintenance home is an alert/message area only. Hide
  it unless there is maintenance attention needed or setup missing on a vehicle.
  If a vehicle alert row is tapped, it should open that vehicle's page/details,
  not silently switch the active vehicle.
- Last service information uses professional helper text, not separate
  exact/estimated selection controls. Tell the user they can enter an exact date
  or an honest estimate. Do the same for odometer/mileage.
- Basic/advanced mode is not a segmented selector. Use a compact disclosure row
  such as Show Advanced Details with a chevron. Tapping it expands/collapses the
  advanced details.
- Advanced details are item-specific and paired fields must sit side by side
  when space allows. Examples: Engine Oil uses Oil type + Oil weight.
  Transmission Fluid uses Fluid family + Specification.
- Mileage interval and time interval are paired. They belong on the same row
  when space allows: mileage on the left, time on the right.
- Custom miles and custom months must not be separate always-visible fields.
  Custom belongs inside the same dropdown/selector as the presets. Choosing
  Custom opens numeric entry with the device keypad.
- Engine oil interval presets: 3,000 miles, 5,000 miles, 7,500 miles,
  10,000 miles, plus Custom. Time presets: 3, 6, 9, 12 months, plus Custom.
- Transmission/coolant/longer-service mileage presets must include practical
  values such as 25,000, 50,000, 75,000, 100,000 miles, plus Custom where
  appropriate.
- Threshold UI must use solid functional bands, not text fields. Mileage and
  time thresholds should be paired on the same row when space allows.
- Mileage threshold bands:
  Green: 900+ miles.
  Yellow: 601-900 miles.
  Orange: 301-600 miles.
  Red: 0-300 miles.
- Short time threshold bands for engine oil and items under one year:
  Green: 31+ days.
  Yellow: 21-30 days.
  Orange: 11-20 days.
  Red: 0-10 days.
- Long time threshold bands for items over 12 months:
  Green: 43+ days.
  Yellow: 29-42 days.
  Orange: 15-28 days.
  Red: 0-14 days.
- Threshold section must explain that these bands control when the maintenance
  item changes color on the maintenance home screen.
- Item-level notifications belong above the threshold section on each
  maintenance item because each item may need different reminder behavior. The
  item setup can show in-app, push, and audible reminder toggles as UI state. Do
  not wire OS permission requests in this UI pass.
- App-level notification channel rules and permission explanations belong in
  maintenance settings later. Do not remove item-level reminder preferences.
