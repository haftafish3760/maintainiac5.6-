- Bluetooth vehicle identification is opt-in assistance. The phone may detect a
  configured vehicle Bluetooth connection and suggest or switch the active
  vehicle according to the user's setup, but the user must be able to review and
  correct the vehicle.
- Automatic trip starting based on Bluetooth/GPS context is a future opt-in
  assistance mode. It must never be required for manual mileage tracking and
  must not silently create trusted odometer records.
- GPS-assisted trip tracking should suggest what mileage could be, while the
  physical odometer remains the confirmed source of truth.

Source: visible user message, 2026-07-23 first GPS setup and device policy pass

- Trip tracking must use device detection/capability information when choosing
  GPS sampling behavior. Battery level, charging state, device capability, and
  user-selected accuracy mode must influence how often location is requested.
- Users can choose high/precise accuracy, with clear battery tradeoffs. If the
  phone is plugged in, higher accuracy can be less restrictive, but it still
  requires explicit user opt-in.
- Low-battery protection should stop or avoid GPS when the phone is below the
  configured threshold, unless the user has explicitly allowed tracking below
  that threshold or the device is charging according to the approved policy.
- Do not require the battery to charge back above 15% before GPS can work when
  the phone is already plugged in and the user has opted into tracking. Avoid
  punishing users with confusing battery gates.
