Maintainiac master-blueprint discussion notes — 2026-08-03

Status: Capture notes only. These are not yet a complete blueprint or an
implementation instruction. Do not add requirements that were not stated in
the discussion.

Product direction
- A new clean build is expected after the app is defined screen by screen.
- Maintainiac 5.6 Active and 5.7 Active are reference/extraction sources;
  their existing expense, GPS, OCR, and other work must not be casually thrown
  away.
- Define each screen, its systems, and its Release-1 purpose before beginning
  the new build, so the new build does not repeat the current accumulation of
  disconnected changes.

Daily command-center direction
- The dashboard is the command center for the whole app.
- The dashboard must change state between before the user's workday and during
  the user's workday.
- The dashboard is the before-workday and during-workday command center, not a
  settings page. During a workday it must show what is happening throughout the
  app and record the day's activity.
- There is a full daily record screen (working name: Workday/Today), not merely
  a GPS trip tracker. It contains everything that happened that day, including
  GPS coordinates/routes, stops, mileage, expenses, payments, receipts, jobs,
  and later corrections or entries the user forgot to add at the time.
- Stops include delivery stops, pickups, and contractor/landscaper stops.
- A calendar is not required on every screen if users can reach the appropriate
  daily record and add or correct items for the day.

User platforms
- Gig drivers and delivery drivers share one platform.
- Contractors use a different platform experience.
- The same underlying Workday record may support both, but dashboards and
  screen options must prioritize each platform's needs.

Module direction stated so far
- Expenses serve both platforms, with role-specific fields and views where
  appropriate.
- Materials/inventory is contractor-only; gig/delivery users do not need an
  inventory module.
- Contractor-only areas mentioned: jobs, estimates, invoices, crews, and
  materials/inventory.
- Gig/delivery-specific areas mentioned: driving activity, stops, earnings,
  payouts, mileage, and vehicle/trip expenses.
- Invoices, estimates, and payments are a major app area. An estimate proposes
  work; an invoice records completed/billed work; payments let independent and
  gig users keep track of money received.

Product framing
- Each major screen/module is comparable to a standalone Play Store app (for
  example mileage tracking, daily activity tracking, invoices/estimates/
  payments) but Maintainiac connects them into one record-keeping product.
- The app is a record-keeping tool for anyone using a vehicle for business,
  especially users who mix personal and business vehicle use.
- The purpose is to maintain the records a user needs to provide to a tax
  preparer or CPA.

Quality and launch direction
- The user cannot afford a release that loses early users because core features
  fail or required records are missing. The intended quality bar is a heavily
  tested commercial product before release, not a quick launch followed by
  routine repair.
- The product must be affordable and must not hide a user's data behind a
  paywall.

Potential storage and subscription direction (not decided yet)
- Evaluate a free storage allowance such as 50 MB or 100 MB. The user is
  considering 100 MB for the first 100 users as an initial experiment.
- Automatic trip tracking may be a subscriber feature. Free users may receive
  three automatic-trip-tracking days each month so they can evaluate it.
- The user does not want a standard two-week trial that ends before users see
  a durable reason to keep using the app.

Map and Workday direction
- The Workday system needs a map/GPS component so delivery drivers and
  contractors can enter and track their stops for the day.

Expense-system direction
- Expense is a full application area, not merely a receipt attachment screen.
- It must help users understand expense measures such as gas cost per mile,
  total expense per hour, what they spend money on, and their largest spending
  categories.
- Expense is also the source of cost intelligence for the Dashboard. It needs
  to calculate and expose fuel economy, fuel cost per mile, overall cost per
  mile, and other user-selected spending measures when the required records
  exist. It must not invent a metric when the necessary mileage, odometer, or
  fuel data is absent.
- Job photos do not belong to expenses. Expenses retain receipt evidence;
  job photos belong to jobs and their related estimates or invoices.
- The product principle is real user control: users control their experience,
  their records, the information shown to them, and how each workday, expense,
  job, or other record is managed.
- Source-of-truth rule: a record the user enters or explicitly confirms is
  authoritative. GPS, maps, OCR, sensors, imported evidence, and any future
  assistant feature may only provide suggestions, evidence, warnings, or
  calculations. They must never silently create, change, or confirm a user
  record.
- Odometer and GPS calibration requirement: at End Day, show the user a
  suggested ending-odometer value derived from the current GPS estimate, but
  require the user to enter or confirm the actual odometer. That confirmed
  odometer overrides GPS. The app must use the difference across valid
  completed trips to adjust future GPS suggestions per vehicle, including when
  tire or speedometer changes affect mileage. This adjustment must never alter
  past confirmed records or silently change a user-entered odometer.

Open questions to decide later
- Exact names and final navigation for Dashboard, Workday, and daily timeline.
- The exact dashboard buttons, cards, and priority order for gig/delivery
  versus contractor users.
- The final Release-1 boundary for each module.

Build-planning decision
- Do not attempt to straighten out the present application wholesale before
  defining the replacement. First create a complete blueprint for every screen
  and supporting system: what it does, what appears on it, how a user moves
  through it, the data and processes it requires, and how those processes
  accomplish the result.
- After that blueprint exists, audit the current application for reusable,
  verified systems and carry those into the clean build rather than rebuilding
  expensive work blindly. Examples include OCR, receipt processing, GPS,
  expense calculations, storage, invoices, and PDF generation.
- The user is relying on the build process to translate product intent into
  technical architecture and implementation details; the blueprint must make
  those details explicit before the new application is created.

Dashboard layout decision
- Neither of the present gig-driver Dashboard layouts is approved: neither the
  pre-day Dashboard nor the active Workday Dashboard is a layout to carry
  forward. Preserve only reusable underlying systems such as the Workday
  record, user confirmation workflow, odometer records, and GPS evidence;
  redesign both screens from the blueprint.

Gig Dashboard: pre-day requirements being defined
- The gig-driver pre-day Dashboard must make basic weekly answers immediately
  visible: take-home money for the week after expenses, and the amount the
  user has personally spent out of pocket that week.
- Tools and actions must be discoverable and visible. Do not hide important
  actions behind obscure menus or require users to hunt for controls.
- The current screen has some of the needed information, but its layout is
  not approved; this is a presentation and hierarchy redesign, not a reason
  to remove the weekly information.
- The headline is period-to-date, not a completed-paycheck view. It must show
  what the user has made and spent so far in the currently configured pay
  period, even when the period is still in progress.
- Required setup behind a period-to-date amount: schedule type (weekly,
  biweekly, monthly, custom, or platform-specific), anchor start date, time
  zone, income sources, inclusion rules for tips/bonuses/adjustments, pending
  versus paid status, included business expenses, personal-payment status,
  reimbursements/refunds, and the rule used to calculate net.
- The Dashboard must label an expected next payday as an estimate, show
  missing records as not recorded rather than inventing a zero, and retain
  user-editable historical periods after confirmation.

Shared screen-header and navigation rules
- The present active-day Dashboard header is rejected because it repeats the
  active vehicle in a second Workday Context block. Rebuild the top row rather
  than carrying that block forward.
- Every major screen uses one consistent top header: hamburger menu on the
  left, a clear title describing the screen in the center, and a settings gear
  on the right when that screen has settings.
- The top context area must let the user change both the active vehicle and
  active work profile. Do not show a duplicate vehicle label merely to expose
  context.
- A gear always opens settings for the screen currently visible. Dashboard
  settings belong to the Dashboard; Calendar settings belong to Calendar; the
  same rule applies to every other app area and secondary screen.
- Every secondary screen requires an explicit back control. Android system
  Back and iPhone back-swipe gestures must return safely and consistently.
- The same header pattern applies throughout the app. For Dashboard, the
  pre-day and active-day states use the same header structure; their available
  settings may differ because they are different screen states.

Responsive layout requirement
- The new app needs an explicit shared breakpoint system for phone, tablet,
  and desktop. Each tier must use an intentional composition, not a stretched
  phone screen. The Dashboard blueprint must define the layout for all three
  tiers before implementation.
